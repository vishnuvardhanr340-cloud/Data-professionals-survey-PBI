# Insights

Based on all 473 respondents (no slicer selected).

## Salary page

- **Role drives pay:** Data Scientists earn the most on average ($91.6K), about 66% more than Data Analysts ($55.1K). Data Engineers average $60.4K, and students and job seekers $27.9K.
- **Education helps:** respondents with a Master's average $61.4K versus $50.5K for a Bachelor's, about 21% higher.
- **Most pay is modest:** the median salary is $53K, and 43% of respondents are in the lowest band (under $40K). Only about 10% earn above $105K.
- **Age matters:** 35-44 year-olds earn the most ($68.3K), nearly double the 18-24 group ($36.0K).
- **Gender:** women average $55.4K and men $53.4K, so the salary-gap measure shows -3.7%. The difference is small and the sample is 73% male, so it should not be read as a general pay gap.
- **Location:** US respondents average $77.5K versus $32.2K for India, roughly 58% lower.

**Recommendation:** for someone entering data, moving toward data science and getting a Master's show the largest pay differences in this survey.

## Happiness and career page

- **Overall happiness is middling:** the average is 5.2 out of 10.
- **Pay is the weak spot:** salary satisfaction is lowest (4.2), then upward mobility (4.8). Coworkers are rated highest (5.8), then work/life balance (5.7).
- **Role matters:** Data Scientists are the happiest (6.4 overall); students and job seekers the least (3.2).
- **Career switching is common:** 60% moved into data from another field, including 72% of Data Scientists and 66% of Data Analysts.
- **Breaking in is not easy for everyone:** 32% found it difficult or very difficult, rising to 50% for students and job seekers.
- **Job priorities:** 48% say better salary matters most, then remote work (23%), work/life balance (20%) and culture (9%).

**Recommendation:** newcomers should expect a harder entry and lower satisfaction early on; the data points to salary and growth, not culture, as what improves it.

## Limitations

Self-reported 2022 survey data; salaries are band midpoints; some groups are small (18 Data Scientists, 3 PhDs).
